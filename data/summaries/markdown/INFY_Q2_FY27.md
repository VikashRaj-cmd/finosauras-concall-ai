# Infosys Ltd — Q2 FY27 Earnings Call Summary

## TL;DR

*   Revenue growth was softer than expected at 1.5% YoY, with AI revenue being a standout performer at 10.5% of total revenue and growing 29% sequentially.
*   Large deal wins were strong, reaching $5.2 billion in Q2, with 62% net new, and the company sees a solid foundation for future growth.
*   Operating margins remained stable at 21%, but future margins will be impacted by compensation hikes and acquisition-related amortization, partially offset by Project Maximus.
*   Full-year revenue growth guidance remains at 1% to 2% in constant currency, with operating margin guidance maintained at 20% to 22%.

## Management Commentary

*   Revenue growth for Q2 was at 1.5% YoY and 1.2% sequentially in constant currency terms, which was below expectations.
*   AI revenue was a standout at 10.5% of total revenue, growing 29% sequentially, reflecting differentiated market positioning and client confidence.
*   Large deals were another standout at $5.2 billion for the quarter, including two mega deals, with H1 total large deal wins showing 28% YoY growth.
*   Client spending remains cautious due to geopolitical tensions, inflationary pressures, and shifting technology priorities, tempering discretionary spend.
*   The company continues to gain market share, especially where program economics align with stakeholder interests, and remains a preferred AI strategy and deployment partner.
*   H2 margins will be impacted by compensation hikes, softer revenue seasonality, continued sharing of AI driven productivity benefits, and ramp-up of large deals, partly offset by Project Maximus.
*   The company feels good that the organic business will be in a good shape in the next year, supported by strong deal wins and a healthy pipeline.
*   AI revenue growth is secular and expected to compensate for deflation in the legacy book.
*   The company is competitive in the market for building AI capacity and has a tremendous advantage in AI compared to many peers.

## Guidance & Outlook

*   **Revenue Growth Guidance:** 1% to 2% YoY in constant currency terms for the full year.
*   **Operating Margin Guidance:** Remains the same at 20% to 22% for the full year.
*   **Effective Tax Rate for the Year:** 29% to 30%.
*   **Target for FY27 Freshers Hiring:** Approximately 20,000.
*   **Compensation Hikes Rolling Out:** Most employees effective October '26, next phase effective January '27.
*   **Fiscal '27 Margins:** Expected to be above the 21% midpoint, with the lower end of guidance assuming further deterioration in macro.

## Segment Performance

*   **AI Revenue:** 10.5% of total revenue, growing 29% sequentially. AI revenue is new revenue and business transformation driven by AI is new revenue. Economics of the AI business are pretty decent, and AI deals are getting good pricing.
*   **Large Deals:** $5.2 billion for the quarter, with 19 wins and 62% net new. Second highest quarterly TCV in the last five years. H1 total large deal wins: $8.8 billion, YoY growth of 28%. One mega deal for over $2 billion with a large international company for seven years, net new, expected to contribute over 1% of revenue growth in the coming financial year. Second mega deal for $1 billion for a renewal. Robust large deal signings in Financial Services of more than $3.5 billion in Q2.
*   **Financial Services:** Did well. Saw growth momentum across global and regional banks, cards and payments, capital markets and Europe. More than $3.5 billion in large deal signings in Q2.
*   **Manufacturing:** Continues to face near-term pressures, including lower revenue from a large client. Parts of Manufacturing also looked strong.
*   **EURS:** Continues to see stable demand with client spending concentrated on targeted, high impact programs. Expected to grow higher than the company average.
*   **Retail:** Sequential growth primarily impacted by lower third-party revenues. Subdued environment and macro uncertainty resulted in soft consumer demand, leading to ramp-downs in some clients. Consumer tech and Logistics are most impacted. Softened this quarter, hurt by lower discretionary spending and smaller third-party.
*   **Communications:** Witnessing a challenging demand environment with clients maintaining tight control over discretionary spend and prioritizing cost efficiency. AI led driven productivity expectations are putting pressures on pricing and delivery model.
*   **Hi-Tech:** Impacted by lower third-party revenues as well as reduced spend in a few large clients due to cost pressures. Softened this quarter, tied to a US client ramp down. For the most part is focused on one client in the US.
*   **Energy Utilities:** Did well.
*   **Healthcare:** Looked strong.
*   **Mortgage Sector:** Expected to see a slowdown due to increased rate hike expectation.

## Notable Q&A

### Question: Can you see next year organic growth, which is flat this year? Do you see some recovery? Are headwinds still emerging or are we largely past them?

**Answer:** With the two big movements of this quarter, first on the AI, which is looking very strong at 29% growth quarter on quarter. And that builds on something that has been going on, strong growth over the last few quarters. And the mega deal win that we shared, which gives us support for next year, plus the pipeline that we see on the mega deals and large deals overall, we feel good that the organic business will be in a good shape in next year.

### Question: What is the business need to run a high level of subcontractors? And what is stopping us to bring it down to pre-COVID levels?

**Answer:** There is an endeavor to bring our subcontractor cost down over a period of time. It has come down and then slightly it has inched up again. It is a factor of at what utilization you are at, what the volume growth comes in, what kind of skills that you need and what geography that you need. Sometimes, some of these programs where you need them, maybe short term, immediate kind of a demand, and that is why the subcontractor comes in. Our endeavor is to keep it in a tap, keep it within a broad band.

### Question: Is it a trade-off between higher utilization and running higher subcontracting cost? And may not necessarily be margin accretive if we bring that down also.

**Answer:** If you are able to bring the subcontractor down by 1%, it gives you around 15 bps to 20 bps on margin. So yes, to that extent, there is opportunity, but it will never come down to zero. Even in the best years, we have been in the range of 5%, 6%.

### Question: What changed most in the growth guidance revision versus last quarter? What are the largest drivers?

**Answer:** Pricing pressure on the back of competitive intensity, which has increased. Pricing expansion that we were expecting at the beginning of the year has not materialized. Softer than expected revenue growth or a volume growth in the quarter. Slower discretionary spend in some of the sectors, especially the Retail and HiTech. Lower revenue from offshoring. A large deal that we walked out of or we did not pursue.

### Question: On the AI driven productivity, has it worsened since the start of the fiscal year, meaning clients are demanding more productivity in just the past six months? Or is it more so just getting more prevalent across your client portfolio as AI programs scale?

**Answer:** The expectation on the productivity is about similar. Some parts of our portfolio have already been exposed to it and others are getting exposed to it. There is a broader uptick, as AI revenues has also gone from 8% to 10%, there is just more awareness of this, especially in some industries, and so we see that coming into play, but the intensity does not seem to have changed.

### Question: How to read the Banking strength versus Hi-Tech weakness for you and at the sector level? Is this the second round of deflation at Hi-Tech? And can it spread to other sectors or Banking strength is more relevant and a positive for other sectors?

**Answer:** Banking, Financial Services has done well. Energy Utilities, has done well. Parts of Manufacturing, Healthcare also looked strong. Hi-Tech is focused on one client in the US. Retail is seeing a change in the segment vis-a-vis the client footprint. There are a lot more industries or sectors, which are showing good traction, good adoption of the AI and also good discretionary like financial services, energy utilities. That is more of what is likely to happen across the entire set of industries.

### Question: Can you see next year organic growth, which is flat this year? Do you see some recovery? Are headwinds still emerging or are we largely past them?

**Answer:** With the two big movements of this quarter, first on the AI, which is looking very strong at 29% growth quarter on quarter. And that builds on something that has been going on, strong growth over the last few quarters. And the mega deal win that we shared, which gives us support for next year, plus the pipeline that we see on the mega deals and large deals overall, we feel good that the organic business will be in a good shape in next year.

### Question: Analyst asked about potential risk to the midpoint of guidance or push/pulls for the next two quarters.

**Answer:** Management stated entering seasonally weaker part of the year with furloughs and lower working days, and that guidance is based on multiple models with baked-in uncertainty at the lower end and improvement at the upper end.

### Question: Analyst asked about the nature of work in a net new mega deal and margin implications during ramp-up.

**Answer:** Management stated it's a business transformation opportunity using AI, and no real margin impact on transition this year.

### Question: Analyst asked if the mega deal involves asset and people takeover.

**Answer:** Management stated no asset takeovers, but some people takeover.

### Question: Analyst asked what would keep fiscal '27 margins above the 21% midpoint and what would push it below, runway left on Maximus, and other levers if savings slow.

**Answer:** Management discussed past margin performance, headwinds from compensation and amortization, partial offset from Maximus, and confidence in guidance.

### Question: Analyst asked to size the impact of Hi-Tech and Retail softening and if it's fully run through or expected further.

**Answer:** Management stated they do not give vertical specific guidance and that headwinds are baked into the overall guidance.

### Question: Analyst asked if pricing pressure is broad-based or client/sector specific, and incremental pricing assumptions for H2.

**Answer:** Management stated demand for productivity benefits from AI is passed on, leading to pricing pressure across clients and industries. For guidance, lower end assumes slight deterioration, upper end assumes improvement.

### Question: Analyst asked about the margin profile of AI revenues and large deals.

**Answer:** Management stated large deals have similar margin profiles, and they do not share AI revenue margins externally but are getting good pricing.

### Question: Analyst asked if AI use cases are informing and helping win large transactions, and if they are shorter duration.

**Answer:** Management stated AI elements are in almost every large transaction, driving significant value.

### Question: Analyst asked about the evolution of go-to-market on the AI side and how services like foundry are resonating.

**Answer:** Management described a multi-component approach involving partnerships, dedicated individuals within segments, delivery specialists, trained personnel, and an architecture group.

### Question: Analyst asked for year-on-year growth rate for AI revenue.

**Answer:** Not provided in the excerpt.

### Question: Analyst asked for year-on-year growth rate on GenAI part.

**Answer:** Management stated they are not sharing externally but can make assumptions based on provided data, noting strong year-on-year growth.

### Question: Analyst asked for a broad number or calculation on the portion of the order book that has undergone deflation and the remaining portion yet to undergo deflation.

**Answer:** Management explained their internal model based on deal size and contract duration but did not share external numbers.

### Question: Analyst asked if AI-led deals have lower tenure and faster conversion.

**Answer:** Management stated they are not specifically calling out velocity and duration but that large deals have significant AI components.

### Question: Analyst asked about the conversion of deal wins into revenues and if leakages have peaked.

**Answer:** Management stated conversion ratio is stable and productivity impact is being managed, with some parts of the portfolio having already witnessed it.

### Question: Analyst asked where they are in the AI adoption cycle compared to the cloud and digital cycle.

**Answer:** Management stated they have a decent handle on new AI revenue and feel good about it, with some understanding of productivity benefits and discipline on deals.

### Question: Analyst asked if new AI revenues will have higher margins and profit per employee than the company average.

**Answer:** Management stated that business transformation driven by AI gets better value and that the economics of the business are pretty decent, but did not give explicit margin numbers.

### Question: Analyst asked if the bulk of the 1% impact from a manufacturing client will come in the fourth quarter and have a full-year impact next year.

**Answer:** This was asked to Jayesh Sanghrajka.

### Question: Sandeep Shah asked about the AI led strategy standing out versus peers and what differentiates it.

**Answer:** Salil Parekh explained the distinct approach to the addressable market, the Topaz Fabric with foundation models and data protection, strong partnerships, extensive certification and training, and leading market analyst ratings.

### Question: Keith Bachman asked if customers are in-sourcing more due to AI.

**Answer:** Salil Parekh stated they do not see a difference because of AI and that other factors may trigger in-sourcing, while AI can also lead to clients having them do tasks more efficiently.

### Question: Keith Bachman asked about the competitive landscape changing in H2 CY26 and into CY27, and why their results were different than a large provider like Accenture.

**Answer:** Salil Parekh mentioned consistent organic YoY growth over the last few years, focus on AI becoming real, higher offshore percentages affecting revenue, disciplined deal selection, and a strong competitive advantage in AI. Jayesh Sanghrajka added that offshoring and not pursuing a large deal together meant 1.8% to 2% for the year.
